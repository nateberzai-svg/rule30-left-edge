### B. C27 under the fair coin (proposal B; EXACT check of the rulebook's reading, MEASURED split)

The rulebook's C27 (every run of two or more waits starts after a one-row move or a "tight" move)
holds in the machine with the back stripe read as r_{d−1}, one left of the stripe r_d that the move
crosses (Theorem B(c)), and "tight" as white on the jumped rows M_d+1 .. M_{d+1}−2 and black on
M_{d+1}−1, the landing row free (with the landing row required white, as the rulebook's sentence can
be read, the law fails on the seed itself: 17,979 counter-runs). On the seed 90,000–800,000: 281,372
moves of 2+ rows, 99,830 tight (35.5%); runs of 2+ waits after one-row / tight / non-tight moves
5,542 / 36,228 / 0; P(2+ waits | tight) = 0.363. The coin machine at depth 64, 10⁸ diagonals:
39,668,006 long moves, 14,055,677 tight (35.43%); runs of 2+ waits 782,328 / 5,092,735 / 0
(13.3% and 86.7%, the rulebook's 13% and 87%); P(2+ waits | tight) = 0.3623; bad share (runs of 2+
waits per cycle) 0.10814 against the seed's 0.10806. The deep-coin model reproduces C27's mechanism
split to three decimals, and the proved half of C27 (never after a non-tight move) holds in
25.6 million machine cases, as it must.

### C. The stationary bad share of the fitted chain is an identity (proposal A)

The m = 2 chain on contexts (two symbols, offer), with the decision kernel and the next-offer kernel
both estimated by counts from the seed's event sequence 20,000–800,000 (2,358 states), has
stationary P(move) 0.54360, speed 1.31952 and bad share 0.10806, the empirical values to five
decimals. This is not a result but an identity: the stationary law of a chain fitted by counts on a
stationary sequence is the empirical context frequency, so "the invariant measure of k ≥ 2" is the
measured share again with the sampling noise hidden in the kernels. The chain becomes informative
only with an offer model that is not the data (C25: random offers give 1.184), and the deep-coin
machine already is the model with the real wall and no fitted table: its bad share, 0.1081 on 54
million cycles, is the number proposal A is after. Where the automaton sits against C10's 27% is
therefore known: at 0.108, with the gains measured, not proved.

### H. The stall tail: the margin of the wait bound, which is the inequality Conjecture H rests on (MEASURED, with an extrapolation)

The rulebook's C17 (every run of waits is shorter than the stripe period, k ≤ P − 1; longest 14 to
4,000,000) is the inequality Theorem H* needs. The machine's forty billion diagonals give the tail
of the stall distribution to 10⁻¹⁰ per cycle (`scripts/stall_tail.py`; the one stall of 43–62 per run
is the crystal's ignition delay at the start and is excluded):

| P(k) per cycle, period 32 (15.4·10⁹ cycles) | 7.27e-6 | 1.59e-6 | 2.59e-7 | 3.53e-8 | 6.09e-9 | 8.4e-10 | 1.9e-10 | — |
| P(k) per cycle, period 64 (6.5·10⁹ cycles) | 7.30e-6 | 1.61e-6 | 2.61e-7 | 4.23e-8 | 6.90e-9 | 1.8e-9 | — | 1.5e-10 |

The two tails are the same law: the stall distribution does not depend on the period, only the bound
does. The decay is geometric, P(k+1)/P(k) = 0.433 at both periods (fit over k ≥ 12). Longest stalls
seen: 24 at period 32 against the bound 31, 26 at period 64 against the bound 63. Extrapolated:

    period 32: P(k ≥ 32) = 6.7·10⁻¹³ per cycle, one stall of 32 per 2.7·10¹² diagonals;
    period 64: P(k ≥ 64) = 1.9·10⁻²⁴ per cycle, one stall of 64 per 10²⁴ diagonals.

The seed's period-32 regime is 2.1·10⁹ diagonals long (10.231d I(vii)), so under the coin model the
bound k ≤ 31 fails somewhere in it with probability about 8·10⁻⁴; the period-64 regime, at least
10¹¹ long, cannot see a stall of 64; and every later regime has a larger bound against the same tail.
Consistency with the seed: the machine's P(k ≥ 14) is one per 7.2·10⁵ diagonals, so the seed's
4,000,000 should hold about five stalls of 14 or more and none of 20 or more (one per 1.5·10⁸),
which is what it holds (longest 14). Reading: the one inequality Conjecture H rests on is safe at
every depth with a margin that grows with the period; what it is not is proved, and the machine can
say nothing about a stall the universal band forbids or allows beyond what the coin sees.

### I. The frozen universe's centre column to 10⁹ (EXACT column, MEASURED tests)

The rulebook's B8: the stripes run back to row 0 form a Rule 30 universe of their own, and its
centre column, b_B(d) = r_d(d), is what ours would be if the wedge reached it. The label recursion
writes it directly (`wall_recursion128 … COLFILE`, 35 s to 10⁹; `scripts/frozen_column.py`,
`scripts/column_memory.py`; logs in `results/frozen_column/`). It agrees with 10.229d's column from
the frame on all 189,999 diagonals 90,001–279,999, and is exact to the doubling at 2,107,985,254
and known up to a global complement beyond it (the doubling's two branches are translates, and the
seed's choice between them is the one bit the machine votes 3:1 rather than unanimously). On
d = 90,001 … 10⁹ (999,910,000 bits):

- density of ones 0.500029, every 10⁸-block within 0.4999–0.5001;
- agreement with the real centre column on 90,001–1,130,000: 0.49973 (chance ½ ± 0.00049, z = −0.55);
  the two columns are unrelated;
- no period p ≤ 10⁷ on the last 10⁸ bits (so any eventual period is above 10⁷ or has a preperiod
  beyond 9·10⁸);
- run lengths geometric: counts within 0.1% of N/2^L for L ≤ 12 and within 1.5% to L = 18
  (249,978,774 runs of 1 against 249,978,115 expected); longest run 32 among 5·10⁸ runs;
- blocks of 8, 12, 16 bits: χ² = 220 / 4,068 / 65,475 on 255 / 4,095 / 65,535 degrees of freedom,
  each within one standard deviation of a fair coin;
- memory (the rulebook's B10 test): the best guess of the next bit from the last k bits scores
  0.50003, 0.50004, 0.50020, 0.50081, 0.50323 for k = 1, 4, 8, 12, 16 on 10⁹ bits, against a fair
  coin's 0.50000, 0.50005, 0.50022, 0.50082, 0.50325 (in-sample bias); on the 1,040,000 bits where the
  real column is available, frozen column / real column / fair coin score 0.5995 / 0.5999 / 0.5994
  at k = 16 and agree to three decimals at every k: neither column has any memory a 16-bit context
  can see;
- autocorrelation: |r| ≤ 2 sd at every lag 1–16, 24, 31–33, 48, 63, 65, 96, 128 (sd 3·10⁻⁵), with one
  exception, lag 64: r = −1.95·10⁻⁴, six standard deviations, a trace of the period-32 stripes two

Reading: a column made of nothing but stripe cells, with no chaos anywhere in its construction,
passes every coin test the real centre column passes, matches the real column at chance, and is
computable in seconds to any depth. Whether it is eventually periodic is the same kind of question
as P1 for the real column, with the same answer so far: no period found, none provable. [A first
predictor run reported 53% at k = 16; that was the in-sample bias of a per-chunk best guess (150
samples per context), caught by the calibration and corrected; the numbers above pool the counts.]

### J. The frontier (EXACT, running)

The label recursion continues on the seed's lineage from its labels at 10¹¹ (final labels of
`results/wall_recursion_logs/wall128_p128_search_fast.txt`) toward 10¹², at 30 million stripes a
second; at the time of this entry it has passed d = 138600000000 with no constant stripe since
2,107,985,254, so the period-64 regime is at least 136.5·10⁹ stripes long. When a fork of even
weight appears, its branch is decided by twenty edge-machine runs started 10⁷ stripes before it
(the front's phase locks within thousands of stripes, E), and the recursion continues with that
bit; at a fork of odd weight the period doubles to 128 and `wall_recursion128` carries on with
128-bit labels, the edge machine with `--top 130`. Every label and every branch to that depth is
then a computation with no Rule 30 in it.
