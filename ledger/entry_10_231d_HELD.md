## 10.231d — The edge automaton: the front closed on one bit per diagonal, and a rule-free transducer on edge events and stripe offers that reproduces the speed (2026-09-24, session d)

Numbering provisional. Scripts `scripts/edge_automaton.py` (the exact window version),
`scripts/edge_transducer.py` (the rule-free version); table `results/edge_automaton_m2_table.npz`.
Data: the 800,000 frame of 10.229d; the frozen universe of the held rule B8 is the background
field B of 10.229d I, and the stripes below are its diagonals r_j.

### A. The exact reduction (PROVED, one line; validated EXACT)

In the diagonal frame D_{d+1}(t+1) = D_{d−1}(t) ⊕ (D_d(t) ∨ D_{d+1}(t)). So diagonals d−1 and d on
rows [M_d − W, M_d + 65] together with the single cell D_{d+1}(M_d − W) determine diagonal d+1 on the
same rows, and since M_{d+1} ≤ M_d + p_d ≤ M_d + 64 (Theorem 17.10(c)) they determine M_{d+1}. Hence
the front is generated by the wall and exactly one input bit per diagonal, the cell W rows below
the front on the new diagonal; at W = 0 that bit is the global input D_{d+1}(M_d) of 10.154c2.
Run with the true bits from d = 100,000 the window machine reproduces the seed's M_d with zero
deviations over 5,000 diagonals at W = 32, and over 3,000 at W = 90 and at W = 4. This version uses
the rule and is recorded only to fix what input the edge needs: one bit per diagonal, at any depth.

### B. The rule-free decomposition (EXACT)

Read the frozen universe only. At diagonal d the stripe ahead makes an offer, g_d = min{t ≥ M_d+1 :
r_d(t) = 1} − M_d, the distance from the front row to the next black cell of stripe d. By Theorem
17.10(c), used here only as the definition of what is read off the frozen data, a move at d has size
exactly g_d (checked: no exception in 434,905 moves). So the edge process is a sequence of decisions
β_d ∈ {wait, move}; the sizes are the stripes' offers. On d ∈ [20, 800,000):

| | value |
|---|---|
| P(move) | 0.5436 |
| mean offer E[g] | 2.2348; P(g = 1,…,6) = 0.368, 0.331, 0.151, 0.075, 0.038, 0.019 |
| speed = P(move) · E[g \| move] | 1.3201 |
| a fair coin independent of the offer would give 0.5 · E[g] | 1.1174 |

The edge declines one-row offers: a move on 40% of them against 63% of any larger offer. The
excess of the speed over 1.117 is therefore two things, the bias of the decision (0.5436 > ½, the

### F. What is derived and what is measured

Derived: the input the edge needs (A), the offer/decision decomposition (B), and the exact laws
of E once proved. Measured: the per-context probabilities of D, hence the speed. The fair-coin
programme of the held rulebook (B9's fractions 3/8 and 27/32) is the attempt to derive those
probabilities; 10.229d and the modes of Corollary 17.9 say where it will and will not close: the
residual half bit of C is the transient's decision, and conditioning on the modes of the waits
takes the k = 3, 4, 6 cases most of the way to determinism (10.229d, this session's message of
record). Registered prediction: the m = 2 table, fitted on the seed, reproduces the speed of every
boundary-driven machine of 10.229d G–I to 0.005, because their fronts are the seed's up to
translation; and it fails on the crystal (b_B), whose front has no moves at all. Kill criterion for
the automaton as a model: a boundary whose ignited front it misses by more than 0.02 in speed.

### G. Blocks: what the rules force over blocks, and why no block argument bounds the speed (EXACT; the reading PROVED in outline)

The rulebook's edge laws (its C5–C8, and E above) are a grammar on edge events, and a grammar
forces conditions on every block of events. The question that decides whether blocks can prove
Conjecture H is: what is the slowest edge the grammar allows? Computed as the minimum-mean-cycle
of the graph whose nodes are the last m symbols (decision, offer) and whose edges are the
transitions observed to 800,000 (`edge_transducer.py` machinery; Karp's algorithm):

| memory m | transitions seen ≥ 1 / ≥ 5 / ≥ 50 times | slowest allowed mean gain, rows per diagonal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 256 / 240 / 199 edges | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| 2 | 1,834 / 1,397 / 788 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| 3 | 8,823 / 5,240 / 1,893 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| 4 | 31,197 / 13,140 / 2,824 | 0 / 0 / 0.143 |

The slowest path is a loop of waits, and it exists at every memory because stall runs of length
13 occur and a window of m < 13 events cannot tell the twelfth wait from the first. Since the true
grammar is at least as permissive as the observed one, no finite-memory rule set on edge events
bounds the speed below: the rules fix the gain of every cycle shape, never the frequency of the
shapes. C10's threshold, cycles with two or more waits under 27% against a measured 10.8%, is the
exact form of what remains.

The same holds for local potentials. A bounded function Φ of the front's neighbourhood with
Φ_{d+1} − Φ_d ≤ J_d − 1 at every step would give M_d ≥ d − range(Φ), which is H with a constant,
from local information alone. Over 200,796 sampled steps (every fourth diagonal from 20,000 plus
every diagonal of a stall run of six or more), with Φ linear in the 102 defect and background bits
of diagonals d−1, d, d+1 on rows F_d − 8 … F_d + 8 (`potential_lp.py`, HiGHS), the best margin is
exactly −1: the optimum is Φ ≡ 0, and no linear potential lowers itself at every wait. This is not
a degeneracy of the features: the neighbourhood is never identical before and after a wait, on any
of 71,199 sampled waits, for windows of 8 to 120 rows on three or four diagonals. And a potential
on the window states themselves exists exactly when the state graph has no cycle of mean gain
below 1, which is the grammar computation again with a larger alphabet; the wall alone admits
stalls of 67 (10.229d D) and the transient realises 13, so every rule-consistent local state
graph contains a slow cycle. Conclusion: no argument over bounded blocks, whether grammar,
automaton or potential, can prove H; every such argument reduces H to the frequency of long
stalls, and that frequency is decided by the transient's half bit per diagonal (C above).

What blocks do give, and should be written up as theorems: the exact cycle accounting (C10), the
exact laws of E and of the rulebook, and the statement that H is equivalent to a frequency bound
with an explicit threshold and a measured margin of a factor 2.5.

(iv) Reading. The fair-coin programme of the rulebook closes, statistically: the edge is the rule
in a window of a few dozen rows below the front, the universal wall, and one independent fair coin
per diagonal injected below the reach of any move. Every percentage in C4–C10 is a stationary
statistic of that finite object and is computable to any precision by simulation (and, in
principle, exactly, as the stationary law of a Markov chain on window states driven by the wall's
phases). What is not closed, and cannot be closed this way, is the seed itself: its input bits
e_d are one fixed sequence, and Conjecture H for the seed is the statement that this sequence is
typical enough for the front it drives to outrun the diagonal, a statement of exactly the shape of
P2 for one explicit sequence, with the two-column theorem's flavour that the sequence is not itself
computable from anything local. That is the precise residue of H: not a rule, not a block, one
coin's typicality.

Registered prediction: the depth-64 coin machine run on the wall of any boundary-driven machine of
10.229d reproduces the same laws (trivially, the wall is the same); run on a *different* wall, the
crystal of a different Rule 30 universe (rulebook B8 shifted, or another seed's wall), it gives a
different speed, and the speed is a function of the wall alone. Kill criterion: two walls with the
same label statistics and speeds differing by more than 0.01.

### I. The speed as a function of the wall; the branch rule at frozen diagonals; the wall as its own machine (PROVED / EXACT / MEASURED)

The registered prediction of H(iv) was tested by running the depth-64 coin machine on other walls.
Doing so needed the walls themselves, and building them produced two exact facts about the wall
that were not in the paper's Part III, one of which supplies the mechanism behind the left-half
universality conjecture (paper, Conjecture "left-half universality", 58/58). Scripts:
`scripts/wall_tools.py` (labels by the recursion of Lemma 18.2, siblings, validation),
`scripts/edge_automaton.py --selfwall` (the machine generating its own wall),
`scripts/wall_recursion.c` (the recursion in C, for depths beyond any frame).

(i) Walls by the recursion alone. The labels are a backward orbit of π (Lemma 18.2): given
(a, b) = (r_{d−1}, r_d), the next label c = r_{d+1} solves c(t+1) = a(t) + b(t) + (1 + b(t)) c(t).
With u* the last row before t at which b(u*) = 1 the solution is closed:

    c(t) = a(u*) + 1 + Σ_{u* < s < t} a(s)   (mod 2),

so when b has a one the periodic solution is unique and its period divides lcm(p_{d−1}, p_d), and
when b = 0 (a frozen diagonal) c(t) = c(0) + Σ_{s<t} a(s) gives two periodic solutions, complements
of each other, of period p_{d−1} or 2p_{d−1} according as r_{d−1} has even or odd weight (Rowland's
parity law, Proposition "period"(d) of the paper). Two consequences. (a) The running maximum of the
period grows only at frozen diagonals, and there only by doubling. (b) At an odd-weight frozen
diagonal the two branches are time-translates of each other by p_{d−1} (c(t + p_{d−1}) = c(t) + 1),
so the wall above such a diagonal is one wall up to a shift of half its new period; only the
even-weight frozen diagonals branch into different walls. In the seed to 800,000 the frozen
diagonals are 2, 7, 28, 399, 53,207, 58,286, 87,866 with weights of r_{d−1} equal to 1, 1, 3, 3, 6,
10, 5: the period doubles at 3, 8, 29, 400, 87,867 and the genuine branchings are 53,207 and
58,286 (E7 and E8 of the paper: the branchings of Gravner's label tree and its leaves "dying by
period doubling" are exactly the even- and odd-weight frozen diagonals). EXACT: rebuilding the
seed's labels from (r_19, r_20) by the recursion alone, taking the seed's branch at the five frozen
diagonals met, reproduces the frame's label at all 799,980 diagonals (`wall_tools.py validate`,
0 mismatches). The closed form agrees with a scalar solver on 3,000 random pairs.

(ii) The branch rule (PROVED). Let d be a frozen diagonal, so D_d(t) = 0 for t ≥ τ_d and
D_d(τ_d − 1) = 1. The frame recursion D_{d+1}(t+1) = D_{d−1}(t) ⊕ (D_d(t) ∨ D_{d+1}(t)) gives
D_{d+1}(τ_d) = D_{d−1}(τ_d − 1) ⊕ 1, whatever D_{d+1}(τ_d − 1) was, and for t ≥ τ_d it gives
D_{d+1}(t+1) = D_{d−1}(t) ⊕ D_{d+1}(t), the recursion the two candidate labels c⁰, c¹ = c⁰ + 1
both satisfy. Writing D_{d−1} = r_{d−1} + Δ_{d−1} and D_{d+1} = c⁰ + Δ_{d+1} on rows ≥ τ_d:

    Δ_{d+1}(τ_d) = Δ_{d−1}(τ_d − 1) + 1 + c⁰(τ_d − 1),   Δ_{d+1}(t+1) = Δ_{d−1}(t) + Δ_{d+1}(t)  (t ≥ τ_d),

so Δ_{d+1} is eventually the constant β = 1 + c⁰(τ_d − 1) + Σ_{t ≥ τ_d − 1} Δ_{d−1}(t), the finite
sum of the (d−1)-defect from row τ_d − 1 up. Hence: (a) τ_{d+1} ≤ τ_d and M_{d+1} = M_d, the front
never moves at a frozen diagonal (this is Theorem B(d) of the paper, β_{d+1} = 0 when r_d ≡ 0);
(b) the label taken is the candidate with

    r_{d+1}(τ_d − 1) = 1 + (parity of Δ_{d−1} on rows ≥ τ_d − 1),

in particular r_{d+1}(τ_d − 1) = 1 when diagonal d−1 settles first; (c) nothing below row τ_d − 1,
the input bit at depth W included, has any part in the branch: it is decided by the phase of the
front against the wall and by the tail of one defect. Checked at the four frozen diagonals whose
front windows the 800,000 frame holds (`branch_rule_check.py`):

| d | τ_{d−1} | τ_d | τ_{d+1} | M_d = M_{d+1} | defect rows of d−1 above τ_d − 2 | parity | r_{d+1}(τ_d − 1) | rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 399 | 501 | 501 | 498 | 500 | {500} | 1 | 0 | OK |
| 53,207 | 71,116 | 71,116 | 71,114 | 71,115 | {71,115} | 1 | 0 | OK |
| 58,286 | 77,909 | 77,910 | 77,910 | 77,909 | ∅ | 0 | 1 | OK |
| 87,866 | 117,324 | 117,324 | 117,324 | 117,323 | {117,323} | 1 | 0 | OK |

In the window machine the branch is read off one cell: above M_d both neighbours are settled, so
the new diagonal equals the candidate that agrees with it at row M_d + 1.

(iii) The wall as its own machine (EXACT). With `--selfwall` the window machine reads only the two
labels (r_{D0−1}, r_{D0}) of its source, generates every later label by the closed form of (i),
and at a frozen diagonal takes the branch of (ii) from its own cell D_{d+1}(M_d + 1). Run in
'true' mode (true input bits) on 600 diagonals around each of 399, 53,207, 58,286, 87,866, it
generates the seed's labels with 0 deviations and the seed's front with 0 deviations, reading the
branches 0, 0, 1, 1 (0 = the solution with c(0) = 0). The edge automaton of A–H is therefore
closed with nothing external: its state is the window and two labels, its input is one bit per
diagonal, and the seed enters only through the initial window and the initial pair of labels.
The earlier wall-file runs crashed at the sibling lineage's frozen diagonal 291,256 because the
file's arbitrary branch there contradicted the branch the machine's state dictates; with
`--selfwall` the machine runs past every frozen diagonal of any wall.

(iv) The mechanism of left-half universality (EXACT on six machines). By (ii), the branch at a
frozen diagonal depends on the front only through its phase τ_d modulo 2p_{d−1} (and on one
defect tail). The six boundary machines of 10.229d G–H have fronts equal to the seed's up to the
translations s_b = −72, −75, −58, −68, +7, +52 ((01)^∞, (0011)^∞, coin, b ≡ 1, random states of
width 40 and 100) and walls equal to the seed's up to translations s_w read from the patterns
(`phase_mechanism.py`): at d = 60,000 (period 16) s_w = 8, 5, 6, 12, 7, 4 and at d = 150,000
(period 32) s_w = 24, 21, 6, 28, 7, 20, and in all twelve cases s_b ≡ s_w modulo the period. So
every machine's front sits at the same phase against its wall as the seed's front against the
seed's wall, and the branch rule then returns the seed's branch at every frozen diagonal: this is
why "Rowland's other possibility" at 53,208 is realised by none of the paper's 58 initial words
and none of the seven boundaries here. The congruence s_b ≡ s_w is itself implied by the cell-by-
cell identity of the front windows under the translation (10.229d G(iii)), so the conjecture's
frozen-diagonal half reduces to the universality of the front already measured, and the open part
is the universality of the front itself.

(iv′) The phase congruence as a proposition (PROVED from the measured identities). Proposition.
Let a left machine have, beyond some diagonal, the seed's wall up to a translation s_w
(r'_{d+t₀}(t) = r_d(t − s_w)) and the seed's front up to a translation s_b (M'_{d+t₀} = M_d + s_b).
Then s_b ≡ s_w modulo the period, in every period regime. Proof. Theorem B(c) of the paper (the
landing rule): at every jump diagonal d, M_{d+1} is the first row above M_d at which r_d has a 1.
The machine jumps at the same diagonals (its jump sequence is the seed's), so Theorem B(c) for the
machine reads r_d(M_{d+1} + δ) = 1 and r_d(t + δ) = 0 for M_d < t < M_{d+1}, with δ = s_b − s_w:
the seed's own wall must satisfy the landing rule shifted by δ at every one of its jump diagonals.
Finite check on the seed (`front_800k_derived.npz`, the frame's labels): the shifted landing rule
holds at all 37,086 jump diagonals of the period-16 regime (20,000–87,866) for δ ≡ 0 only, the best
other residue holding at 26.1% (δ = 6) and the mean at 23.4%; and at all 385,784 jump diagonals of
the period-32 regime (90,000–800,000) for δ ≡ 0 only, the best other residue at 26.1% (δ = 15), the
mean at 24.7%. ∎ So the frozen-diagonal half of left-half universality is a corollary of the
universality of the front (10.229d G–H) and of the universality of the wall (Proposition 18.6),
